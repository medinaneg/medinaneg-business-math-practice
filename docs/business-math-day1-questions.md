## Step-by-step solutions (shown after submission)

### Question 1 — A (₱600)

Commission = percent × sales.

- \(5\% \times ₱12{,}000 = 0.05 \times 12{,}000 = ₱600\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **B (₱240):** Uses 2% or misreads 5% as ₱240.  
- **C (₱12,005):** Adds 5 pesos instead of taking 5% of sales.  
- **D (₱60,000):** Divides by 5% or multiplies by 5 instead of finding 5% of 12,000.

---

### Question 2 — B (₱450)

Hourly pay = rate × hours.

- \(₱75 \times 6 = ₱450\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱81):** Adds 75 + 6 instead of multiplying.  
- **C (₱375):** Uses 5 hours instead of 6.  
- **D (₱525):** Uses 7 hours or adds an extra hour by mistake.

---

### Question 3 — C (₱3,100)

Gross pay = fixed salary + commission on sales.

- Commission: \(4\% \times ₱15{,}000 = 0.04 \times 15{,}000 = ₱600\).  
- Total: \(₱2{,}500 + ₱600 = ₱3{,}100\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱600):** Commission only; forgets weekly salary.  
- **B (₱15,000):** Uses sales total as pay.  
- **D (₱17,500):** Adds full sales to salary instead of commission only.

---

### Question 4 — A (Store A, ₱12 per pen)

Unit price = total price ÷ number of items.

- Store A: \(₱120 \div 10 = ₱12\) per pen.  
- Store B: \(₱70 \div 5 = ₱14\) per pen.  
- Lower unit price: Store A.

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **B:** Correct unit price for B but wrong conclusion (B is not cheaper).  
- **C:** Divides pens by price (\(10 \div 120\)) instead of price by pens.  
- **D:** Copies Store A’s unit price but assigns it to Store B.

---

### Question 5 — B (₱50 for one 250 mL bottle)

The task is **lowest cost for 250 mL**, not best unit price on a larger bottle.

- One 250 mL bottle = **₱50**.  
- One 500 mL bottle = **₱85** (more than she needs and costs more).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A / D:** Chooses 500 mL because “unit price is better per mL,” but Maria only needs 250 mL.  
- **C:** Buys two small bottles when one is enough (unnecessary extra cost).

---

### Question 6 — B (₱100)

Selling price = cost + markup (in pesos).

- \(₱80 + ₱20 = ₱100\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱60):** Subtracts markup instead of adding.  
- **C (₱400):** Treats ₱20 as 25% of cost and multiplies wrong (\(80 \times 5\)).  
- **D (₱25):** Divides 80 by 20 or confuses markup with profit percent.

---

### Question 7 — B (₱450)

Discount amount = percent × original price; sale price = original − discount.

- Discount: \(10\% \times ₱500 = ₱50\).  
- Sale price: \(₱500 - ₱50 = ₱450\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱490):** Subtracts only ₱10 instead of 10%.  
- **C (₱50):** Gives discount amount only, not sale price.  
- **D (₱550):** Adds 10% instead of subtracting.

---

### Question 8 — B (₱1,500)

Down payment = percent × cash price.

- \(15\% \times ₱10{,}000 = 0.15 \times 10{,}000 = ₱1{,}500\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱8,500):** Finds amount **financed** (remaining balance), not down payment.  
- **C (₱150):** Moves decimal—computes 1.5% instead of 15%.  
- **D (₱10,150):** Adds 15 pesos instead of 15%.

---

### Question 9 — A (₱600)

Monthly payment = financed amount ÷ number of months.

- \(₱7{,}200 \div 12 = ₱600\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **B (₱720):** Divides by 10 months instead of 12.  
- **C (₱12,000):** Uses original laptop price, not financed balance.  
- **D (₱840):** Adds down payment back into the amount divided.

---

### Question 10 — B (₱360)

Extra cost = installment total − cash price.

- \(₱2{,}760 - ₱2{,}400 = ₱360\).

**Why wrong answers are tempting**

- **A (₱240):** Subtracts in wrong order or uses monthly payment by mistake.  
- **C (₱2,760):** States installment total, not the **difference**.  
- **D (₱5,160):** Adds cash and installment totals instead of finding the difference.
